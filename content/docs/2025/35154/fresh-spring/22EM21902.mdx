    en: Foundations of Economics (1) mainly analyzes individual behaviors and the economic consequences arising from their interactions, and accordingly proposes relevant microeconomic policies to improve the efficiency of resource allocation. Students are expected to master core concepts such as incentives, supply and demand, costs, utility, market structures, and market failures. They should also be able to apply the learned theories to analyze microeconomic issues in areas like consumer behavior, producer behavior, and social welfare, and put forward solutions based on this analysis. Through this course, students will not only grasp basic microeconomic theories but also enhance their logical analysis ability, problem identification ability, and conflict resolution ability by repeatedly applying the economic analysis paradigm. Additionally, they should deeply understand the goals, motivations, and effects of individual decisions, analyze market equilibrium, social welfare, and the effects of public policies within the supply-and-demand analysis framework, and gain a thorough comprehension of different market structure types, their characteristics, and equilibrium outcomes. Furthermore, students are required to deeply understand relevant knowledge such as cost theory, consumer behavior theory, and social equity issues, apply relevant economic theories to analyze real-world problems, and put forward well-founded viewpoints.
