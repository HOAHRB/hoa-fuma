    en: This course explores the practical applications of integral calculus in physics and geometry. Students will learn to recognize and address problems in science, technology, and engineering through the use of integral calculus, building confidence in applying these mathematical tools to real-world scenarios. The curriculum covers the foundational theory of integral calculus, including interval functions, additive functions, and the key theorem of integral calculus. It provides a general algorithm for reducing suitable problems to integration and offers simple examples for initial understanding. In geometry, students will apply integrals to calculate areas between curves, arc lengths, surface areas of revolution, volumes of revolution using disk and shell methods, and centroids. The course also introduces the 1st and 2nd Pappus–Guldinus theorems and explores the use of polar coordinates in these calculations. In the realm of mechanics and physics, students will learn to compute moments of inertia and understand concepts like work and energy through integration. The course emphasizes reducing natural phenomena descriptions to forms yielding quantitative results, supported by practical examples across various scientific fields. Additionally, the course delves into statistical applications, teaching students to calculate the average value of a function, variance, and standard deviation using integrals. It also introduces the use of Dirac delta function and Heaviside step function in solving problems involving point-like objects. Through problem-based and interactive teaching methods, supplemented by multimedia tools, students will develop a deep understanding of how to apply integral calculus to diverse problems in geometry, natural science, and statistics.
