    en: 'The course aims to develop students'' ability to solve problems using the theories and methods of basic topology, while also laying the necessary theoretical foundation for subsequent courses. The course will have two parts: The first part focuses on basic notions of point-set topology, the second part will introduce students to the beginnings of algebraic topology. In the first part, students will learn to use the core concepts of convergence and continuity in topological spaces, as well as connectedness and separation axioms, compactness, and compactifications of topological spaces. We will also study various ways of making new topologies from old ones such as product, sum, and quotient topologies. We will also touch upon more specific tools such as uniform convergence and uniform spaces, as well as the Baire category theorem. These tools and ideas are used in nearly every mathematical field, for instance, in functional analysis, advanced measure and probability theory, and hard analysis. In the second part, students will be introduced to the methods of attaching groups to topological spaces in order to gain information about these spaces. Thus, they will learn to use concepts such as homotopy and fundamental groups in their mathematical arguments. These tools will enable students to abstractly analyze the structural properties of spaces and lay the groundwork for advanced courses like differential topology, functional analysis, advanced algebraic geometry and advanced group theory. As applications, we will show how to compute the fundamental groups of some spaces and we will also show how these tools can be used to prove important theorems like the Brouwer fixed point theorem and the fundamental theorem of algebra.'
