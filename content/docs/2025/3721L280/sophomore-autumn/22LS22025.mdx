    en: 'The Experiments of Genetics A is an experimental course designed to complement the Genetics (A) lecture series. It aims to deepen students'' understanding of theoretical course content and cultivate exploratory research thinking by teaching classic genetic experimental designs and concepts, alongside practical genetic laboratory techniques. The course comprises 9 experimental modules spanning various branches of genetics, including classical genetics, quantitative genetics, population genetics, molecular genetics, and epigenetics. Classical genetics experiments primarily involve three projects: rearing the model organism Drosophila melanogaster (fruit fly) and observing its mutants and life cycle; conducting a series of fruit fly crosses; and observing Drosophila salivary gland chromosomes. These projects enable students to familiarize themselves with this common genetic model organism, understand the methods and applications of observing salivary gland chromosomes, and master the fundamental principles of Drosophila hybridization. Human fingerprints, the skin ridge patterns located on the fingertips, exhibit distinct individual specificity and high stability. Certain abnormal dermatoglyphics are clearly associated with genetic disorders; dermatoglyphic analysis can be used for preliminary screening and auxiliary diagnosis of genetic diseases, particularly chromosomal disorders. As a polygenic trait, the study of human fingerprints offers an entry point into quantitative genetics. Humans possess multiple blood group systems, with ABO being one commonly encountered in daily life. Among the four blood types (O, A, B, AB), while A and B types correspond to multiple possible genotypes, the O and AB types allow their genotypes to be directly inferred from their phenotypes. This characteristic facilitates the assessment of population genetic equilibrium. Molecular genetics, a core technical field in modern genetic research, focuses on key methods for gene manipulation and functional analysis. The related experimental projects cover core workflows including gene amplification and recombination, vector construction, as well as transformation and screening. These projects help students master molecular cloning techniques and develop comprehensive abilities in genetic engineering experimental design and data analysis. Epigenetics is the branch of genetics that studies heritable modifications of gene expression and regulation that do not involve changes in the DNA sequence itself, exploring the processes and mechanisms from genotype to phenotype. Genomic imprinting is a key focus of research in this branch. This experiment uses a specific gene sequence in mice as the subject, employing DNA from the maternal parent, DNA from offspring, and cDNA from offspring as materials. Its objective is to detect the presence of parent-of-origin specific expression (i.e., monoallelic expression from only one parent, with the other allele silent) at SNP sites within this sequence, thereby investigating the potential existence of genomic imprinting.'
