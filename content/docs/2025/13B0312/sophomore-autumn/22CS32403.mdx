This course is designed for students aspiring to become future leaders in technology, aiming to bridge the final gap between AI algorithmic theory and its deployment in physical-world products. We will systematically explore the methodologies for implementing and optimizing machine intelligence on edge devices constrained by power, computation, and memory resources. This is not merely a course on advanced technology; it is an intellectual and practical journey focused on creating intelligent physical entities and addressing critical national technological challenges. This course is committed to building a comprehensive framework of knowledge and skills. At the theoretical level, we will begin with the hardware architecture of embedded systems, conducting an in-depth analysis of the operational principles of ARM processors and AI accelerator chips. Key concepts such as parallel computing architectures and systolic arrays will be examined to understand the trade-offs between performance and power consumption. Grounded in the embedded Linux operating system, students will master its kernel, file systems, process management, and essential command-line operations, establishing a solid foundation for advanced software development. On the AI application front, the course will focus on the design principles of lightweight neural networks and deliver systematic instruction on core optimization techniques, including model compression, quantization, and pruning. Students will gain proficiency in mainstream industrial-grade inference frameworks such as TensorFlow Lite, NVIDIA TensorRT, and Intel OpenVINO, mastering the critical skills for efficient AI deployment on edge devices. Ultimately, the course will cover the holistic architectural design of embedded intelligent systems, integrating hardware interfacing, sensor data processing, and AI model deployment to cultivate comprehensive capabilities in intelligent application development. The course emphasizes the deep integration of theory and practice, adopting a pedagogical approach that combines heuristic and seminar-style teaching with a flipped classroom model, supplemented by rich engineering case studies to stimulate student interest. The laboratory component will be conducted in a Linux development environment and consists of four core experiments (totaling 8 lab hours): Embedded Development Environment Setup, Performance Analysis of AI Inference Frameworks and Model Deployment, Sensor Data Acquisition and Processing, and Lightweight Neural Network Model Implementation and Inference Evaluation. Working in teams, students will experience the entire project lifecycle—from system design and algorithm deployment to hardware integration and software debugging—thereby honing their full-stack engineering capabilities by tackling real-world challenges. The assessment for this course is designed to comprehensively evaluate students'' integrated abilities. The final grade is composed of homework assignments (10%), in-class quizzes (10%), laboratory reports (30%), and a final group project report and defense (50%). Particular emphasis will be placed on the systems thinking, innovative spirit, and teamwork demonstrated in the final project. Students are expected not only to build a robust intelligent system but also to clearly articulate their design philosophy, technical implementation, and critical reflections through a high-quality report and presentation, thus aligning their personal and professional development with the nation''s technological demands in the field of embedded AI.'
