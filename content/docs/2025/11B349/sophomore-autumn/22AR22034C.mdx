Reconstructing the paradigm of architecture and decorative arts research from a cross-cultural perspective, through a historical interpretation of material culture from the mid-18th century to contemporary times, revealing the complex interaction between power, technology, and aesthetics in the process of globalization. By integrating the multidimensional perspectives of material cultural history and spatial philosophy, the focus is on analyzing ideology in architectural practice. The course emphasizes the establishment of a cross regional comparative framework within a diachronic context, examining the standardized impact of Enlightenment rationality on architectural aesthetics. The course objectives include: to have a basic understanding of the technical, social, political, cultural, and economic factors involved in the production of building environments at a fundamental level, and to have a broad understanding of some of the major structures in contemporary architectural history. At the level of skill mastery, familiarize oneself with the major historical, political, and social facts of the past three centuries. Elucidate the constantly changing significance of architecture for those who entrust, design, construct, use, preserve, and demolish buildings. At the level of appreciation and analysis, apply historical methods and critical theory to the study of the history of architecture and decorative arts in the Western world from the mid-18th century to the present day.'
