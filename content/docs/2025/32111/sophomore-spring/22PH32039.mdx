    en: Introduction to Particle Physics is an elective course for undergraduate students in the School of Physics. The objective of this course is to enable students to master the basic theoretical framework, research methods and ways of thinking of particle physics. Cultivate students' ability to discover and solve problems, as well as the scientific spirit of seeking truth from facts and being brave in innovation. Particle physics is the branch of physics that studies the fundamental particles that make up matter and the laws of interaction between them. All known elementary particles can be described using a theory known as the Standard Model of particle physics. In this theory, the three basic interactions of electromagnetic, strong, and weak in nature are realized by passing the corresponding gauge bosons. This course will introduce students to the elementary particles that have been discovered so far and what their properties are. Since accelerators and colliders are commonly used in particle physics, particle kinematics will also be taught. The interaction processes between particles can be described by Feynman diagrams, and this course will teach students how to draw Feynman diagrams of some typical physical processes. In addition, the application of symmetry in particle physics will be emphasized, especially how some important conserved quantities play a huge role in the discovery of particles, and how symmetry breaks lead to the establishment of weak interaction theory. Finally, the quark model, how quarks and leptons are involved in weak interactions, and flavor mixing will be taught.
