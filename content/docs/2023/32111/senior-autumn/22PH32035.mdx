Plasma physics was developed both to provide an understanding of these naturally occurring plasmas and to advance the pursuit of controlled nuclear fusion. Plasma science is now used in a number of other practical applications, such as the etching of advanced semiconductor chips and plasma decontamination. This course will introduce the basic principles of plasma physics and its generation and applications. The course is suitable for students whose primary interest is in gas discharges, plasma materials processing, ionospheric physics, space plasma physics, or astrophysics. Theoretical topics include single-particle motion, kinetic theory, magnetohydrodynamics, and collisional effects. Many different types of plasmas are included (e.g., weakly ionized plasmas, dusty plasmas, non-neutral plasmas,) with a focus on non-relativistic, quasi-neutral, weakly coupled plasmas. Introduces the basic theory of plasmas, plasma generation, diagnosis, and related applications through a step-by-step derivation and a self-contained mathematical approach.'
