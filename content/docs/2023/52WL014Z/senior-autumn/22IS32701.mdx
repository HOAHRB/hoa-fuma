    en: With the continuous involvement of numerical simulation methods in scientific research, and due to the immaturity of the methods themselves, it has constantly raised questions about the accuracy of simulation results, especially for complex measurement systems involving flow and heat transfer processes, such as nuclear reactor systems. Compared to numerical simulation methods, experimental methods are often used as the "final" means of verification, and few people doubt experiments and naturally tend to treat experimental results as "determined true values", especially when numerical simulation results appear together with experimental results. In fact, experiments often inevitably face uncertainty issues, but such issues are often intentionally or unintentionally overlooked in the practical process. For any precision measurement research work, there are uncertainties in theoretical analysis, numerical simulation, and experimental research. Therefore, the uncertainty of measurement models in theoretical analysis, the uncertainty of algorithms in numerical simulation, and the uncertainty of data processing in experimental research are all the root causes of uncertainty in the final measurement results. This elective course is aimed at measuring objects including but not limited to geometric quantities, thermodynamic quantities, mechanical quantities, electromagnetic quantities, radio quantities, optical quantities, time/frequency quantities, chemical quantities, acoustic quantities, and ionizing radiation quantities. It introduces the applications and limitations of various measurement model modeling methods, introduces the principles, significance, and reliability of GUM and Monte Carlo methods, and introduces the principles of data processing methods such as least squares and regression analysis Meaning and hypothesis testing, using mathematical methods such as Taylor series, probability theory, mathematical statistics, parameter estimation, and system identification to estimate the total measurement uncertainty, and implementing digital simulation of measurement uncertainty using computer software. The entire teaching process can enable students to have a deep understanding of error theory knowledge, comprehensively understand mathematical modeling ideas, systematically master numerical simulation algorithms, proficiently use data processing methods and computer simulation tools, thereby enhancing their innovative ability to apply what they have learned.
