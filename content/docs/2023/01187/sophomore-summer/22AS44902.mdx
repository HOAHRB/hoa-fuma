    en: 'This course is optional that is suitable for the undergraduate students in engineering discipline， also in ultraprecision instrumentation and fabrication engineering. Ultra-precision optical surfacing and metrology is one of the most important parts in the engineering domain. More and more large aspherical surfaces with extremely high specifications are adopted in modern optical system design. Manufacturing and testing large aspheric and freeform surfaces pose many challenges. Therefore, the development of space optics, astronomy, aerospace and laser fusion strongly depend on the manufacturing accuracy of the optical components. The aim of this course is to review the current situation in the developments of fabrication and metrology techniques for large aspheric and freeform surfaces. In this course, Prof. David Walker will introduce the current main polishing and figuring techniques for the fabrication of large aspheric parts that are from the developments of his own research team to other important processes including: University of Arizona stressed lap, early UCL stressed lap, bonnet polishing, fluid-jet polishing, MRF, reactive-plasma, ion-figuring etc. As a case study, the project of ‘The European Extremely Large Telescope – prototype mirror segments’ that has been led by Walker’s team will be introduced including the significant role of segmentation in extremely large telescope, the challenges in fabrication of segments and metrology, and the importance of support technique. Metrology and the NASA Hubble Space Telescope also will be introduced as a Case study.'
