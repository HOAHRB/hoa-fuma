    en: Importance of the solid-state physics is primarily determined by a wide scope of technological applications. Most part of our functional materials, instruments, vehicles and various devices include the key details made of solid state matters. Furthermore, existence of crystals which are the ground-state forms of any solid material opens the way to relate the macroscopic properties of bulky bodies with peculiarities of their atomic and electronic structures. The course of solid state physics is aimed at promoting a better understanding the physical lows which determine the mechanical, thermal, electrical and optical properties of the crystalline materials. It includes presentation of basic concepts of the experimental and theoretical crystallography, the solid state mechanics, thermodynamics, optics and electronics. The course represents the fundamentals of various experimental technics used in the solid state physics, such as X-ray diffraction, IR and Raman spectroscopy, inelastic neutron scattering and ultrasonics. Especial attention will be paid to the structural phase transitions which govern the magneto and ferroelectricity. Brief introduction to the modern computational solid state theory will be accompanied by an acquaintance of the students with available program packages and databases.
