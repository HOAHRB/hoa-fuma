Mid-infrared lasers have important applications in trace gas detection, non-destructive ultrasonic testing of composite components of large aircraft, laser wind radar for human eye safety, laser biomedical treatment and countermeasures. This course is a further expansion of the basic courses such as "Laser Technology", and is a typical example of the theoretical knowledge of optoelectronics to guide the design of novel laser devices. At the same time, it is also an extension of the "Mid-infrared Laser Technology Practice" course in the summer semester of the third year, which is a learning process that first has the sensory and prior knowledge of the relevant devices, and then conducts in-depth discussions and redesigns for specific applications. For designers and fabricators of laser devices, it is often the material that determines the device performance. There are many kinds of mid-infrared laser materials, and how to select the appropriate materials and combine the reasonably designed resonant cavity type to achieve high-performance laser output is an important problem in the design and preparation of new band lasers. The course first summarizes the characteristics of the mid-infrared spectral region, the development and application of mid-infrared laser materials and devices, and the main body of the course first introduces the crystal symmetry, single crystal growth and light wave propagation characteristics in the crystal, focusing on the materials, devices and applications of thulium-doped, holmium-doped 2 micron solids and fiber lasers, and learns the light-wave coupling equation in nonlinear optical crystals and the basic principles of 3~5 micron wavelength-conversion devices.
