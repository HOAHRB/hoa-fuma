This course starts with the development of biological visual systems, systematically teaching the elements, components, and problems in machine vision imaging systems such as image noise and degradation, as well as their causes and solutions. The aim is to enable students to understand and master the principles of machine vision devices and the current development status of modern computational optics; This course focuses on the evolution of brain processing capabilities through the collection of large amounts of information in biological vision. The development of machine vision has driven the development of artificial intelligence, and classic methods in machine vision such as image filtering and edge detection have corresponding "algorithms" in the human visual system. It enables students to understand that the development of modern deep learning constantly draws inspiration from the human brain. In the future, intelligent visual detection methods and instrument designs can still draw inspiration from the treasure trove of biology. This course aims to re teach key classic algorithms in machine vision from the perspective of modern artificial intelligence, with the aim of enabling students to understand classic cases of deep learning processing visual information, such as object segmentation and recognition, and thus gain a general understanding of machine vision. In order to strengthen the connection between theory and practice, the course provides a detailed overview of practical cases applied to major engineering projects; Setting up actual machine vision software to complete designated detection and measurement tasks; Set up a visual calibration verification experimental stage; Established a machine vision application design phase.
