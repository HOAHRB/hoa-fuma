    en: '“Climate Change and Transportation Infrastructure: A GIS Approach” is a professional elective course for undergraduate and graduate students in Road, Bridge and River-Crossing Engineering, Transportation Engineering, Traffic Equipment and Control Engineering, and related fields. Using QGIS as the main teaching platform, the course introduces GIS methods for climate data processing, spatial analysis, transportation infrastructure risk identification, vulnerability assessment, and resilience-oriented decision support. Under global warming and more frequent extreme weather events, transportation infrastructure faces increasing uncertainty. Rising temperatures may affect pavement service conditions, asphalt material selection, PG grade distribution, structural performance, and maintenance costs, while extreme precipitation may cause urban road flooding, traffic disruption, and drainage system failure. The course helps students understand how climate change challenges traditional engineering assumptions and why spatial risk analysis is essential for future infrastructure planning, design, maintenance, and management. The course consists of eight parts. It first introduces the relationship between climate change and transportation infrastructure, including climate uncertainty, temperature rise, extreme rainfall, pavement deterioration, flooding, and infrastructure failure risks. It then covers GIS and QGIS fundamentals, including spatial data types, raster and vector data, coordinate systems, projections, and common formats such as Shapefile, GeoTIFF, and NetCDF. The course also explains GIS representation and processing of climate data, including temperature, precipitation, extreme climate indicators, climate station data, time series data, and future climate scenarios such as RCP and SSP. Key application modules include GIS analysis of pavement temperature change and asphalt performance, spatial mapping of PG grade changes, pavement degradation risk zoning, GIS-based urban flooding analysis under extreme precipitation, SWMM-GIS data interfaces, road inundation and risk mapping, and overlay analysis of road function and flooding vulnerability. The course further introduces vulnerability and resilience assessment methods, multi-factor spatial overlay models, climate risk zoning, and the concepts of Fail-safe and Safe-to-fail. Comprehensive case studies are used to connect data, models, maps, and engineering interpretation. After completing the course, students should be able to use QGIS for basic spatial data processing, understand the impacts of climate change on pavements, urban roads, and drainage systems, conduct preliminary GIS-based risk and resilience analysis, and propose spatial decision-support schemes for transportation infrastructure under climate uncertainty. The course assessment includes class performance, assignments, and a final examination.'
