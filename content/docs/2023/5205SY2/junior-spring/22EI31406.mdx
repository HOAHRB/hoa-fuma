    en: The Microwave Passive Circuits course is a core requirement for students specializing in electromagnetic fields and wireless technology. It forms the foundation for subsequent studies in wireless communication systems and electronic systems, playing a crucial role in developing students' comprehensive analysis, design capabilities, and problem-solving skills for complex engineering challenges. The course primarily covers topics such as transmission line models and equivalent circuits for non-uniform regions of transmission lines, methods for implementing reactive elements in microstrip circuits, and the principles, performance metrics, and design methodologies of typical microwave passive components like lumped element matching networks, distributed element matching networks, filters, power dividers, and directional couplers. Special emphasis is placed on planar structures of microwave passive circuits. Practical components of the course include the simulation and design of impedance transformers, filters, power dividers, and directional couplers, as well as the operation of vector network analyzers and the measurement of microwave passive devices. These hands-on projects are representative and practical, addressing the limitations of theoretical classroom teaching and enhancing students' understanding through active participation. By completing this course, students will acquire the skills to correctly select, use, and design common microwave passive circuits. They will be able to perform equivalent circuit analysis on microwave passive components, use electromagnetic simulation software for design, and conduct experimental testing with modern instruments. Furthermore, students will be equipped to develop or select modern tools to simulate and predict specific needs in the electronic information field, understand their limitations, and apply these tools to analyze complex engineering problems effectively.
