    en: The term "big data" originally originated in the internet and IT industry. However, with the completion of the "Human Genome Project," it spurred a revolution in the biotechnology industry. High-throughput sequencing technology developed rapidly, endowing life science research with powerful data output capabilities, including genomics, transcriptomics, proteomics, metabolomics, and other biological data. These omics data are characterized by large Volume, Variety, Value, and high Velocity. Omics big data is a cutting-edge field in bioinformatics, and the R language is a key for biology students to understand and master omics big data. The most distinctive feature of this course is its novel content. The course mainly teaches the basic principles and R language tools needed for cutting-edge omics big data analysis. It includes the analysis techniques of high-throughput biological big data, such as nucleic acid databases, protein sequence databases, protein structure databases, multi-omics sequencing data (genome sequencing, transcriptome sequencing, metagenome sequencing), and gene regulatory network data. The content covers all the current mainstream biological databases and retrieval tools. In addition, it uses the R programming language as a medium to provide students with the ability to analyze biological big data using computer programming languages. The primary educational goal is to cultivate undergraduate students' practical abilities in cutting-edge scientific research. By analyzing the omics big data of new generation sequencing technologies, it enhances students' abilities to analyze omics big data and solve real biological problems. In this process, it aims to cultivate students' creativity, exercise their logical thinking abilities, initiative in learning, and the ability to solve problems on their own. Meanwhile, through the design of course projects and reports, it fosters students' teamwork skills. This course is aimed at undergraduate students in bioinformatics, specifically targeting students from medical and health schools with weak foundations in computer programming. It significantly increases hands-on practice opportunities and selects the R language, the computer programming language most akin to natural language, for the analysis of high-throughput multi-omics data. Even students with no background in computing can master various retrieval and analysis tools and use the R language to write their analysis problems.
