    en: 'It systematically explores the theoretical framework, core technologies, and industrial implementation pathways for the deep integration of artificial intelligence (AI) and additive manufacturing (AM, commonly known as 3D printing). Structured around the "design-process-production-service" lifecycle, the course establishes a comprehensive knowledge system spanning intelligent material design, process parameter optimization, production control, and AI-driven quality inspection. It emphasizes how machine learning, deep learning, digital twins, and generative design technologies break through traditional manufacturing constraints to propel additive manufacturing toward intelligent paradigms characterized by enhanced precision, efficiency, and reliability. The curriculum addresses both fundamental theories and industrial applications: Theoretically, it provides in-depth analysis of AI-empowered AM principles, including neural network-based materials genomics for accelerated material discovery, multi-objective process optimization via reinforcement learning, melt pool dynamics monitoring through computer vision, and virtual manufacturing workflows enabled by digital twins. Practically, cross-industry case studies – such as lightweight aerospace component design, personalized medical device fabrication, and automotive part topology optimization – demonstrate AI''s transformative role in redefining manufacturing processes. Examples include generative adversarial networks (GANs) automatically creating structures that meet mechanical and lightweight requirements, time-series prediction models anticipating thermal stress deformation in selective laser melting (SLM), and transfer learning-enabled defect recognition with limited training data. The course highlights the innovative "data-algorithm-physics" trinity methodology, systematically explaining digital thread construction strategies for AM – covering multi-source heterogeneous data acquisition (process parameters, sensor data, microstructural images), feature engineering techniques, physics-informed neural network (PINN) development, and cloud-edge collaborative decision systems. For AM-specific challenges like anisotropy control, residual stress mitigation, and support structure optimization, dedicated modules investigate engineering solutions: hybrid intelligence systems integrating thermodynamic simulations for closed-loop parameter optimization, defect knowledge graphs employing causal reasoning to enhance process stability, and more. In smart manufacturing contexts, the course analyzes AI-driven smart factory ecosystems comprising self-aware equipment, adaptive path-planning algorithms, and autonomous MES decision systems, while exploring blockchain applications in product traceability. Forward-looking discussions address paradigm shifts emerging from AI-AM convergence, including mass customization business models, distributed manufacturing networks, and in-space fabrication – illustrated through NASA''s orbital 3D printing robots and GE''s integrated fuel nozzle production. To cultivate interdisciplinary innovation capabilities, the course adopts a "theory-simulation-experiment" tripartite pedagogy, supplemented with Python-based AM algorithm toolkits, Unity3D process visualization platforms, and ROS robotic control modules. Hands-on projects enable mastery of complete skill chains from data acquisition to system integration, including developing intelligent slicing parameter systems for FDM, deep reinforcement learning-based support generation models, and multi-physics coupled digital twins for metal AM. Ultimately, the course aims to develop systems-thinking leaders capable of identifying industrial pain points, designing AI solutions, and evaluating techno-economic viability – reinforced through factory visits, expert workshops, and technology roadmap simulations.'
