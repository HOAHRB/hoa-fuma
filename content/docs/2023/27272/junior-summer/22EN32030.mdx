    en: Membrane technology offers advantages such as high space efficiency, excellent separation precision, and the absence of by-products, making it highly promising for water treatment applications. Integrating multiple membrane technologies or coupling them with conventional treatment processes can significantly improve water quality, enhance operational efficiency, and reduce the footprint of treatment systems. This course centers on water treatment membranes, introducing the scientific principles of membrane separation, comparing the structures and fabrication methods of various membrane materials, explaining the mechanisms and application features of different membrane technologies, and analyzing outstanding membrane-based water treatment processes. The course covers individual membrane technologies including microfiltration, ultrafiltration, nanofiltration, reverse osmosis, forward osmosis, electrodialysis, membrane distillation, membrane bioreactors, and catalytic membranes. Water treatment applications span centralized, household, and portable drinking water systems, wastewater treatment and reuse, seawater/brackish water desalination, and water recycling processes. The course will also introduce the emerging role of artificial intelligence in membrane material design and system operation, such as data-driven performance prediction, operational monitoring, and fault detection, to familiarize students with the integration of digital tools and membrane technology. The basic objective of the course is to enable students to understand the scientific issues underlying membrane technologies, including size exclusion principles, interfacial electrostatic interactions, and solution-diffusion mechanisms. Students will learn to analyze various membrane processes based on thermodynamic and kinetic concepts, master the regulation and maintenance of individual membrane technologies in water treatment, and understand the core structures and fabrication methods of various membrane materials. The advanced goal is to train students to design, construct, or upgrade membrane-based water treatment systems with clear objectives in mind, aiming for multi-stage and circular use of water resources. The course also fosters interdisciplinary thinking across economic, policy, and technical domains, as well as teamwork and collaboration skills. Students will be encouraged to explore cutting-edge approaches, including the potential of AI to support membrane process modeling and optimization, enhancing system intelligence and precision.
