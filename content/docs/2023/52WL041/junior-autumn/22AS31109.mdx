    en: Intelligent system control practice, the core course intelligent systems control practice, is itself one of the important core courses for automation students. Taking automatic control theory and automatic control components as prerequisites, the course serves as a bridge connecting automatic control theories and control systems design practices. The course is oriented to engineering applications and is, therefore, strongly characterized with practicing. The course will lay a sound foundation for the study of succeeding courses, such as process control, guidance and control of flight vehicles, control of unmanned aerial vehicles. The course holds an important position in cultivating students’ overall abilities of analysis and design. The course mainly consists four parts. The first part is to master automatic control components, with a focus on studying motors and their drive control circuits, various types of sensors, etc. The second part gives the methodology of constructing a control system through the analysis of various input signals and performance indices of the control system, and deals with main problems in control system design via, for example, error analysis and methods to reduce control errors, disturbance analysis and suppression methods. In the third part, design procedures for typical control systems, including servomechanisms and regulation systems, are given, together with various practical design examples. The fourth part discusses intelligent control systems, covering basic notions and applications of expert systems and fuzzy control systems. Through the course study, including lecturing in class and real experiments and computer simulations outside class, it is expected that the overall capabilities are cultivated for students in control system design. In addition, it is also hoped that the sense on innovation of students is improved to lay a sound foundation for solving practical engineering problems.
