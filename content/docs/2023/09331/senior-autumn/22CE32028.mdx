    en: This course is one of the important core courses in the "Mathematics, Mechanics, Structures" mainline of the undergraduate teaching plan for civil engineering majors, and it is also a part of the basic mechanics knowledge required for the master's degree entrance examination of "Structural Mechanics" in most universities in the country (in addition to the "Structural Dynamics" course). This course mainly covers the practical engineering content of the preceding "Structural Mechanics" course, including the matrix displacement method, structural stability analysis, and structural limit load calculations. The matrix displacement method focuses on the development and application of computer programs to solve common hyperstatic structures in engineering, gradually cultivating students' ability to transition from manual calculations to computer program calculations. Structural stability analysis mainly targets instability failures in engineering structures (mainly steel structures), teaches two types of stability problem analysis methods and methods for determining critical loads, and serves as the mechanical foundation for the subsequent professional course "Basic Principles of Steel Structures." Structural limit load calculations mainly address the elastoplastic failure of engineering structures (mainly reinforced concrete structures), teaching the evolution process from elastic to plastic failure of structures and the corresponding limit load determination methods, closely related to the content of the subsequent professional courses "Basic Principles of Concrete Structures" and "Seismic Resistance of Engineering Structures." This course primarily aims to cultivate students' ability to apply the knowledge learned in the preceding "Structural Mechanics" course to solve practical engineering problems, with particular emphasis on developing students' ability to use computers to solve complex engineering problems. Through the study of this course, a bridge is built to solve engineering problems using mechanics principles, laying a solid theoretical foundation for students' future structural design and scientific research work after graduation.
