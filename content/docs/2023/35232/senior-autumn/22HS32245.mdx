    en: '"Multilateral Trade System and Global Trade Governance" is an interdisciplinary course that integrates knowledge from international trade, international politics, international law, and other disciplines to explore global trade governance issues from multiple perspectives. It covers both theoretical knowledge such as the origin, development, and core principles of the multilateral trade system, as well as current hot issues and practical cases in global trade governance. This course delves into the historical evolution, legal framework, impact on global trade, and role in global economic governance of the multilateral trade system, analyzes the core functions, basic principles, and reform challenges of the World Trade Organization (WTO), and discusses the future direction of global trade governance in light of regional trade agreements such as RCEP and CPTPP. The course content mainly includes the history and current situation of the multilateral trade system: introducing the development process from the General Agreement on Tariffs and Trade (GATT) in 1947 to the establishment of the World Trade Organization (WTO) in 1995, and the role of the multilateral trade system in global economic governance; the core principles of the multilateral trade system: explaining principles such as non-discrimination, free trade, and transparency, and analyzing how these principles ensure the fairness and freedom of global trade; the challenges faced by the multilateral trade system: discussing issues such as development imbalance, the emergence of new topics, and the rise of unilateralism and protectionism, and their impact on the multilateral trade system; the future development of global trade governance: studying the direction and strategies for reforming the multilateral trade system, and how to build a more inclusive and adaptable global trade governance framework. Through the study of this course, students can gain a comprehensive understanding of the current situation and future development trends of global trade governance, master the practical application of international trade rules, understand the game between globalization and protectionism, and cultivate the ability to analyze and solve complex economic and trade problems.
