    en: 'Course Positioning and Significance "Theory and Governance of Economic Reform" is a specialized elective course offered to undergraduate students majoring in economics and related fields. With the practice of China''s reform and opening up over the past four decades as its core, this course, in combination with typical global economic transformation cases, systematically explores the theoretical basis, policy design, and governance mechanisms of economic reform. The course aims to help students understand the complexity and diversity of economic reform, master the theoretical tools for analyzing reform issues, and cultivate their abilities in policy evaluation and critical thinking. Against the backdrop of the new era, China''s economy faces new challenges such as high-quality development and the construction of a dual-circulation development pattern. This course will provide students with important knowledge reserves and a thinking framework for academic research, policy analysis, or business decision-making. Course Content and Structure The course content is divided into three major modules: Theoretical Framework: It systematically expounds on the core theories of economic reform, including new institutional economics, transition economics, public choice theory, etc., with a focus on analyzing the interactive relationship among institutional change, market-oriented reform, and the transformation of government functions. Reform Practice: It focuses on the phased characteristics of China''s reform and opening up (such as the household contract responsibility system with remuneration linked to output in rural areas, the reform of state-owned enterprises, China''s accession to the WTO, etc.), compares international cases such as the "shock therapy" in the Soviet Union and the structural adjustment in Latin America, and summarizes the experience and lessons of the success and failure of reform. Governance Innovation: It explores cutting-edge issues in modern economic governance, including multi-center governance, the construction of a market environment ruled by law, social fairness, and sustainable development (such as the interest coordination in the carbon peak policy). Teaching Methods and Characteristics The course adopts a diversified teaching mode: Case Study and Discussion: By conducting in-depth analysis of typical cases such as the "Pilot of Shenzhen Special Economic Zone" and the "Tax-sharing System Reform," it guides students to understand the practical constraints of policy design. Policy Simulation: It simulates the scenarios of government decision-making and explores the interest balance and risk control in the reform. Interdisciplinary Perspective: It integrates the dimensions of political science and sociology to analyze the non-economic factors (such as culture and ideology) in the reform. Learning Objectives and Outcomes Upon completion of this course, students will be able to: Master the theoretical paradigm of economic reform and explain the gradual path of China''s reform and its uniqueness. Use methods such as cost-benefit analysis and comparative historical analysis to evaluate policy effects. Write reform analysis reports with practical value and put forward targeted policy recommendations. Target Audience and Extended Value This course is suitable for students who plan to engage in fields such as policy research, public administration, and economic consulting in the future, and also provides research topic directions for those who aspire to pursue further academic studies. The course content will be continuously updated in line with the major reform trends of the country (such as the policy of common prosperity and the construction of a unified national market) to ensure alignment with the needs of the times.'
