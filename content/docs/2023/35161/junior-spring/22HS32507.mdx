    en: This course mainly teaches applied psychology as an interdisciplinary field between psychology and human resource management. On the one hand, it is a branch of industrial and organizational psychology. With the rapid development of industrial and organizational psychology in multiple directions, human resource management has become an increasingly important field of concern in industrial and organizational psychology; On the other hand, as a branch of human resource management, the predecessor of human resource management was personnel management. As people were increasingly seen as "human resources" in organizations, "personnel management" began to be gradually replaced by "human resource management". This course attempts to break the structural framework of basic psychology. Based on a simple presentation of the basic procedures, concepts, and principles of psychological research, it incorporates the basic knowledge and theories of psychology into various functional modules of human resource management. It introduces the most commonly involved psychological knowledge and theories in specific fields, focusing on job analysis, recruitment, training, performance, leadership motivation, and interpersonal relationships in human resource management. Through the study of this course, students can understand and initially master the basic laws and methods of applying psychological knowledge and theory to practice, and then be able to combine psychological theory with practical tools and methods.
