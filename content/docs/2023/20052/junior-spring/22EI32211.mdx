    en: Electronic Countermeasure Technology B is an elective course for electronic information engineering majors. This course mainly teaches the basic concepts, theories, and techniques of electronic countermeasures. The course focuses on radar countermeasures and covers some related knowledge in the fields of communication countermeasures, optoelectronic countermeasures, etc. By combining teaching and experiments, students can learn relevant theoretical knowledge and gain more intuitive understanding through experiments, enabling them to understand the importance of electronic warfare technology in modern warfare and cultivating their ability to solve complex engineering problems in electronic warfare systems. The main content includes electronic reconnaissance, electronic jamming and electronic protection etc. Electronic reconnaissance mainly explains how to intercept, measure, analyze, identify and locate the radiation signal of an enemy equipment, and access to technical parameters, location, type, and deployment information. The content involves basic knowledge of signal environment and signal interception, search based superheterodyne receivers, channelized receivers, basic principles of commonly used direction finding techniques, and localization of radiation sources. Electronic jamming introduces the principles of active and passive interferences and the jammer, as well as the principles and methods that reduce the radar cross section. The content involves covering interference, deceptive interference, interference machine composition, and interference energy calculation. Electronic protection includes electronic anti-reconnaissance, anti-interference technology. In this course, students need to master the basic concepts, the basic principles and the related technologies of radio signal reconnaissance; grasp the principles and techniques of radar interference, understand the basic components of the electronic jammer; master the related technology and basic methods to protect one's own equipment during the electronic warfare.
