Electromagnetic analysis involves solving for the current, electric field, and magnetic field in a system based on electromagnetic field theory, such as Maxwell''s equations. Traditional analytical methods are inadequate for complex electromagnetic problems such as RCS (Radar Cross Section) analysis, stealth technology, and target identification, and experimental techniques cannot provide comprehensive solutions. With the application and development of computer technology, the design process of modern electromagnetic field engineering increasingly relies on computer-aided design. This is computational electromagnetics. Computational electromagnetics is an interdisciplinary course that integrates electromagnetic field theory, mathematical knowledge, and programming skills. It is a critical component of electromagnetic field theory and represents a further understanding and application of this theory. With its strong professional focus and broad scope of knowledge, it equips students with the essential skills for future scientific research, product development, and engineering design. The course content primarily includes four fundamental numerical methods: the finite difference method, the finite-difference time-domain method, the finite element method, and the method of moments. Through theoretical learning and programming practice, students will gain a preliminary understanding of the basic methods in computational electromagnetics. They will learn to apply this knowledge to problem-solving, developing their logical thinking and engineering problem-solving skills. This course lays the foundation for future studies, research, and professional work. It aims to cultivate students'' comprehensive computational abilities and practical application skills, enhancing their overall professional competence to better serve society after graduation.'
