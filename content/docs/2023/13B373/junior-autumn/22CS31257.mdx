Ubiquitous computing represents a novel computational paradigm that integrates computing with communication, sensing, control, and other technologies to enable intelligent, adaptive, and omnipresent monitoring of human and environmental states. The course content is divided into three main sections: positioning technology, sensing technology, and recognition technology. Positioning Technology: Covers outdoor positioning techniques, indoor positioning systems, and inertial navigation technology. Sensing Technology: Focuses on the principles, computational methods, and applications of various sensors, as well as sensor interface technologies and non-sensor-based perception methods. Recognition Technology: Explores visual recognition technologies, RFID, and other identification systems. The Principles of Ubiquitous Computing course emphasizes the integration of theoretical knowledge and hardware expertise. It aims to lay a solid foundation for students pursuing advanced studies or research in IoT, computer science, automation, artificial intelligence, or related fields. Additionally, the course cultivates students'' ability to propose solutions or systems for ubiquitous computing engineering challenges. It equips them with the skills to comprehensively apply acquired knowledge, methodologies, and technologies in experimental design, analysis, and evaluation, fostering problem-solving capabilities in real-world scenarios.'
