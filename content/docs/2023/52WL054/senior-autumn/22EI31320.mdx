    en: '"Surveying" is an essential technical foundation course in the geomatics discipline and a compulsory subject for undergraduates majoring in Remote Sensing Science and Technology. This course aims to equip students with a systematic mastery of the core theories and methods for acquiring, processing, and applying topographic information, as well as to develop preliminary practical abilities and professional competence in solving measurement-related problems in relevant fields. Upon completion of this course, students are expected to achieve the following objectives: Course Objective 1: Master the definition, role, and fundamental principles of surveying, and systematically understand the complete knowledge system from ground point positioning to topographic mapping and application. In terms of knowledge, students should focus on mastering core concepts and principles such as the shape of the Earth and coordinate systems, ground point determination, control surveying, basic knowledge of topographic maps, and fundamentals of photogrammetry. In terms of abilities, students should be able to use this knowledge to analyze and explain the overall technical process and logical relationships of topographic mapping, and possess preliminary skills in interpreting topographic maps and extracting basic information from them. In terms of qualities, by understanding the overall framework of the geomatics discipline, students will develop macro-level systems engineering thinking and cultivate an initial awareness of the precision and standardization required in surveying work. Course Objective 2: Master the principles, methods, and techniques of basic surveying tasks, including elevation measurement, angle measurement, distance measurement, and electronic total station measurement. In terms of knowledge, students should deeply understand the fundamental principles of various measurements, key points of instrument operation, observation methods, and accuracy evaluation standards. In terms of abilities, students should be able to correctly use basic surveying instruments for standardized data collection, possess practical hands-on skills to complete field observations, indoor calculations, and result organization, and be capable of conducting preliminary analysis and evaluation of the accuracy of measurement results. In terms of qualities, through practical training, students will develop a rigorous, truthful, and meticulous scientific attitude, establish safety awareness in instrument operation, and foster a spirit of teamwork.'
