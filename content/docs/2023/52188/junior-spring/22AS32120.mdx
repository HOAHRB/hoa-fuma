    en: 'Game theory has rapidly developed, not only maturing and improving its theories but also expanding its applications. The application of game theory has covered various fields, including economics, biology, social and management sciences, military and decision theory, computer science, artificial intelligence, and especially its mutual influence and promotion with control theory. Game theory and control theory belong to universal disciplines, unlike traditional disciplines such as physics, mechanics, or mathematics. Control and game theory not only aim to understand the world but, more importantly, to actively transform it. There is a profound intrinsic connection between control and game theory: control theory primarily deals with non-intelligent mechanical systems such as machines, aiming to design controls that make objective things operate according to the subjective desires of the controller. On the other hand, game theory can be seen as an extension or development of control, controlling intelligent objects with different objectives, leading to non-cooperative games, and cooperative games where the object must balance its interests. The scope of controlled objects in the future will be broader, encompassing intelligent controlled objects such as unmanned vehicles, robots, and flying machines capable of planning trajectories online. As an interdisciplinary field at the intersection of control and game theory, control theory based on games and game theory oriented towards control is expected to become an extremely important direction of interdisciplinary development, both in theory and application. This course focuses on the study of game theory and is designed for undergraduate students majoring in control. This course aims to enable students to understand and grasp the basic concepts, relevant theoretical methods, and the latest research results in game theory, achieving an interdisciplinary combination of game theory and practical applications. The course emphasizes both fundamental theory and cutting-edge explorations, with specific objectives including: (1) Enabling students to understand and grasp the fundamental ideas, theories, and methods of game theory, allowing them to apply the ideas and methods of game theory in system modeling in the field of automation. (2) Inspiring students to think using game theory methods, applying models, basic theories, and methods of game theory to analyze and solve practical problems, and conducting literature research from a systematic perspective to model and analyze engineering problems of autonomous systems in the field of automation, in order to obtain effective conclusions. (3) Training students to design and develop appropriate solutions based on game theory methods for engineering problems in the field of automation, designing systems that meet specific requirements, and demonstrating innovative thinking in the design process, making students more competitive in their future work and research activities in the field of control. (4) Cultivating students'' abilities to use tools including computer programming, data structure and algorithms through practical lab sessions.'
