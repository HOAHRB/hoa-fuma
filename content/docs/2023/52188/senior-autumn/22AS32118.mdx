    en: The course ‘Vehicle Dynamics and Control' is designed for the development of students in the fields of control science and engineering. It aims to broaden students' horizons, stimulate their research interests, and exercise their research abilities. This course introduces the basic theory of automotive dynamics modeling, tire mechanics, and automotive aerodynamics. It focuses on the driving dynamics and braking dynamics dominated by longitudinal motion, as well as the driving dynamics dominated by vertical motion and the control dynamics dominated by lateral motion. This course combines practical engineering project development experience to teach the dynamics and control related content of vehicle driving, braking, and suspension systems from the aspects of dynamic analysis and modeling, control system analysis and design, and simulation verification. Through the study of this course, students will understand the main content of automotive system dynamics research, become familiar with the basic theories of system modeling, tire mechanics, and aerodynamics, master the modeling and analysis methods of longitudinal, vertical, and lateral dynamics, as well as the impact of three-dimensional dynamics on overall vehicle performance, and understand software related to vehicle computer modeling and simulation. It guides students to learn the methods of using dynamics and kinematics knowledge to establish car models and simulate and analyze their dynamic characteristics, master the basic ideas of car system dynamics analysis and modeling, as well as controller design and application, in order to cultivate students' ability to solve scientific and engineering problems. It enables students to understand the relationship between automotive technology and national economy, environment, society, and sustainable development, understand the future trends and main characteristics of technological development, and lay a solid theoretical, technical, and experimental foundation for their future engineering design work in related fields.
