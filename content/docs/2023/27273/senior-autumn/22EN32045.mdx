    en: With the development of socio-economic factors, continuous industrialization, and urbanization, issues such as resource scarcity, environmental pollution, food and food safety, and ecological imbalance are becoming increasingly prominent, posing a serious threat to human survival and development. How to handle the relationship between humans and nature, economy and ecology, resource utilization and protection, as well as the balance between immediate and long-term interests, has become a crucial concern for scholars, decision-makers, and managers. Against this backdrop, environmental ecological planning and management have emerged. Distinguished from traditional economic-centric planning theories and methods, environmental ecological planning and management focus on the diversity, integrity, and continuity of ecosystems, with the ultimate goal of ensuring and restoring ecosystem health. It provides an opportunity to address the relationship between humans and nature, supporting the realization of a people-centered, comprehensively coordinated scientific development outlook, and the construction of a stable and harmonious society. Environmental ecological planning utilizes interdisciplinary principles from ecology, economics, sociology, and modern scientific and technological methods to manage human actions' impact on the ecological environment. The aim is to balance the conflict between development and ecological environment protection, ultimately achieving coordinated and sustainable development of the economy, society, and ecological environment. Guided by principles from ecosystem science, macroeconomics, and urban and rural planning, environmental ecological planning and management employ methods from various disciplines such as systems science, environmental science, management science, software engineering, operations research, mathematical statistics, and computer technology. The goal is to identify, simulate, and design various ecological relationships within artificial composite ecosystems, ensuring the ecological suitability of resource development and protection. It explores strategies for improving the ecological construction of system structure and function, promoting the sustained and coordinated development of the relationship between humans and nature.
