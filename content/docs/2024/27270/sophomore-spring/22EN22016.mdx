This course is tailored to the characteristics of the Environmental Science and Engineering major, closely linking real-world problems related to fluid mechanics within the discipline. It emphasizes the role of practical case teaching in environmental science and engineering alongside theoretical learning. The course achieves the following objectives through classroom and laboratory teaching: Enable students to understand the basic concepts, principles, and methods of fluid mechanics. Through learning the fundamental theories such as basic fluid concepts, fluid statics, fluid kinematics and dynamics, flow resistance and head loss, dimensional analysis, and similarity principles, students will grasp the basic characteristics and methods of fluid mechanics. Based on these, they will learn the hydraulic calculation methods for processes like pressurized pipeline flow, open channel flow, weir flow, and seepage, gaining the ability to analyze and solve problems in fluid mechanics. Master the experimental methods of fluid mechanics research and acquire basic training in experimental design and skills. Be capable of conducting research on complex environmental engineering problems based on the scientific principles of fluid mechanics and using scientific methods, analyzing experimental results, and deriving reasonable and effective conclusions. Through the study of this course, students will develop the ability to pose, analyze, and solve problems in the field of environmental science, providing them with the necessary theoretical foundation in fluid mechanics for further studies in other specialized courses such as urban water supply and drainage network systems, and water pollution control engineering.'
