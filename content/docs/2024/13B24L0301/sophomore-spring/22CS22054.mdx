    en: With the rapid advancement of artificial intelligence, computer vision has become one of its cornerstone technologies. This course systematically introduces the fundamental concepts, principles, and technical frameworks of computer vision, aiming to cultivate students' practical skills and innovative thinking in image processing, feature extraction and matching, and deep learning-based visual applications. The evolution of computer vision dates back to the 1950s, when scientists began exploring image processing and pattern recognition. With significant progress in computational power and breakthroughs in deep learning algorithms, computer vision technologies are now widely applied across domains such as intelligent surveillance, medical imaging analysis, industrial quality inspection, and autonomous driving, providing essential support for digital transformation. This course covers theoretical foundations and cutting-edge technologies in computer vision, including image formation and representation, filtering and frequency domain analysis, feature extraction and matching, multi-view geometry, optical flow and motion analysis, deep learning applications in vision, and neural rendering with 3D representation. Students will learn how to preprocess and enhance images, design and implement feature extraction algorithms, and build visual recognition systems using modern deep learning frameworks. Additionally, the course introduces frontier technologies such as vision-language models, enabling students to stay informed about the latest academic advancements and industry trends. This course is designed for second-year undergraduate students with prerequisites including calculus, algebra and geometry, and college physics. The course comprises 48 total hours (including 32 hours of theoretical instruction and 16 hours of laboratory experiments). The curriculum progressively increases in complexity, combining theoretical lectures with hands-on experiments to guide students in mastering both mathematical principles and engineering implementations of vision algorithms. Assessment employs a diversified evaluation approach that includes class participation and discussions, programming assignments, laboratory reports, and a final examination. This comprehensive evaluation thoroughly examines students' theoretical understanding and practical application capabilities. As computer vision technologies continue to evolve rapidly, this course is committed to building a(systematic knowledge framework for students while enhancing their analytical skills to solve complex visual problems. Through this course, students will acquire professional knowledge and skills in computer vision that can be flexibly applied in future research or engineering practices. We expect students to maintain curiosity and an exploratory spirit throughout their learning journey, actively engaging in the practice of frontier technologies while contributing their intellect to the ongoing advancement of computer vision.
