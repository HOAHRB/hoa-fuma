    en: '"Modern Landscape Thought" is a historical and theoretical course aimed at second-year landscape architecture students. It is the core of undergraduate education in the history and theory of landscape architecture. "Modern Landscape Thought" primarily focuses on the landscape design activities of the modern era, emphasizing the interpretation of design concepts and the assessment and reflection of their values, rather than just historical materials and styles. The course''s teaching objectives are to lead students to explore the essence of modern landscapes, follow the historical development of modern landscapes, broaden their international perspective, enrich their design language, grasp the main ideas and design techniques of modern landscape design, and enhance their theoretical research and critical abilities. The aim is to enable students to understand and master the basic historical facts of modern landscape development, grasp the fundamental context of landscape development evolution, understand the historical laws and development trends of landscape development, integrate landscape design theories, and create outstanding modern landscape works based on Chinese culture. Objective 1: Broaden horizons and enrich design language; acquire knowledge of social sciences related to landscape architecture planning and design, including philosophy, society, art, environmental behavior, and psychology, as a foundation for work in design, research, and management. Objective 2: Master the main ideas and design techniques of modern landscape design; apply the knowledge from the course to analyze issues related to spatial organization, environmental creation, aesthetic form, behavioral perception, material techniques, and more, to guide design and expression. Objective 3: Integrate landscape design theory; combine ecological, economic, environmental, social, cultural, technological, and artistic factors and their dialectical relationships to propose problem-solving methods and strategies. Objective 4: Enhance theoretical research and critical abilities, cultivate students'' sense of professional social responsibility, and boost cultural self-confidence. Encourage students to develop a sense of self-directed learning, continuously research and explore professional issues in landscape architecture and related fields.'
