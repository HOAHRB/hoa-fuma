    en: '"Game Theory and Intelligence" is a course designed for undergraduate students majoring in future technologies within the School of Aerospace Engineering. The course totals 32 class hours (24 hours of theory and 8 hours of laboratory work), carries 2 credits, and is offered in the 3rd semester (spring). Centered on game theory and integrated with artificial intelligence (AI) technologies, this course aims to cultivate students'' capabilities in decision-making and optimization within the field of intelligent automation. The course content focuses on the core framework of game theory and cutting-edge AI applications, covering fundamental theories of static and dynamic games, games with complete and incomplete information, as well as mathematical solutions of Nash equilibrium and analysis of multiple equilibria. Students will delve into classical game cases (such as the Prisoner''s Dilemma and signaling games) and their mappings in automation, while also exploring AI-driven technical modules, including reinforcement learning (e.g., Q-learning), multi-agent reinforcement learning (MARL, e.g., the MADDPG algorithm), and the role of generative adversarial networks (GANs) in strategy generation. Through case studies (e.g., AlphaGo’s application of game theory) and hands-on laboratory experiments, the course illustrates how AI optimizes decision-making processes, enables multi-agent collaboration, and informs system design in adversarial environments. Additionally, students will be introduced to the potential of large language models in generating game strategies and their real-time adaptability, broadening their technical perspectives and enhancing practical skills.'
