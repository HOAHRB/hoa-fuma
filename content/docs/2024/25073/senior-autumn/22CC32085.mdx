    en: This course focuses on the fundamental theories, key technologies, and applications in intelligent synthesis of Chemistry-Specific Large Language Models (Chemistry-Specific LLMs). It covers core technologies such as molecular representation learning, reaction prediction, and retrosynthetic planning, and integrates cutting-edge practices involving robotic chemists and automated experimental platforms. The aim is to equip students with the ability to use AI tools to solve chemical synthesis problems. The core content of the course includes four main aspects. First, the foundation of chemical large language models will delve into molecular representation methods, model architectures, and principles of constructing chemical datasets, laying a solid theoretical foundation for students. Second, the intelligent synthesis technology module will focus on techniques such as molecular generation and optimization, retrosynthetic analysis, and reaction condition recommendations, helping students master the key skills for intelligent synthesis. Third, the experimental automation section will explore the architecture of automated platforms and high-throughput optimization, enabling students to understand how to enhance the efficiency of chemical experiments using automation technologies. Finally, the ethics and frontier module will analyze dual-use risks, reliability assessment, and the development trends of multimodal large language models, cultivating students' ethical awareness and insight into emerging technologies. The teaching features of this course emphasize a practice-oriented approach. Students will integrate theoretical knowledge with practical applications through hands-on operations of mainstream tools. The course also integrates multidisciplinary knowledge from cheminformatics, machine learning, and automated control, breaking down disciplinary barriers and fostering students' interdisciplinary thinking. Moreover, ethical education is integrated throughout the course, combining the latest research to cultivate students' awareness of responsible AI use. Through this course, students will master the core principles and application skills of chemical large language models, be able to independently complete tasks such as molecular design and reaction optimization, and possess the ability to assess the reliability and ethical risks of AI solutions. This course is suitable for graduate students and senior undergraduates in chemistry, pharmacy, materials science, and related fields, helping them lay a solid foundation for future AI-driven chemical research.
