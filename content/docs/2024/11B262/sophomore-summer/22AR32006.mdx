    en: Course Positioning and Academic Context This course is grounded in the frontier perspective of "Hybrid Ecology" within a post-human context. As climate evolution and digital technologies become deeply entangled, the physical boundaries between traditional natural and artificial environments are rapidly dissolving. Landscape design is facing a paradigm shift from the "static reshaping of environments" to the "deep adaptation to extremes." In this context, this course aims to transcend the visual limitations of traditional spatial design by introducing generative computational art and multimodal Artificial Intelligence (AI) technologies, exploring the deep integration of physical environments and digital media at a macro scale. The course will guide students to re-examine the human-land relationship and establish a "neo-nature" cognitive framework that surpasses traditional carbon-based biology and silicon-based computation. Core Content and Design Methodology The core of the curriculum focuses on the concrete expression and cross-media translation of "Invisible Landscapes." The pedagogy will discard the conventional "visual-centric" approach to design, turning its attention to the invisible ecological and physical force fields within spatial environments—such as Thermal Environment Perception, microclimate wind fields, and the psychological boundaries of social perception. Students will learn to bypass complex underlying coding logic, utilizing cutting-edge AI image, video, and audio generation tools to translate abstract environmental metadata into high-fidelity visual spectacles and immersive audio-visual experiences. Through logical training from "natural pattern extraction" to "generative structure deduction," students will endow data with the spirit of place (genius loci), achieving an elevation from objective environmental elements to cross-media artistic narratives. Teaching Objectives and Expected Outcomes As a pioneering and experimental 16-hour intensive workshop, this course is dedicated to facilitating the students' identity transformation from "spatial beautifiers" to "perceptual interpreters." The course requires students to cultivate a macro-level ecological adaptability vision and become proficient in using generative AI tools for conceptual deduction. Ultimately, on an individual basis, students are required to independently complete and submit a multi-dimensional digital landscape conceptual short film or a cross-media environmental intervention proposal (spanning visual, auditory, and kinetic dimensions) addressing a specific extreme or future climate scenario. This aims to foster students' independent creative capabilities, forward-thinking design logic, and media mastery within the complex hybrid ecosystems of the future.
