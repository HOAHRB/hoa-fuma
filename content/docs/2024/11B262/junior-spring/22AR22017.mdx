    en: 'Automatic Control Principle is the fundamental theory for analyzing and designing HVAC automatic control systems in building environment and energy application engineering. Through the study of this course, students are required to be able to model, analyze stability, accuracy, and dynamic performance of general control systems, understand the comprehensive and calibration tasks of control systems, and have the basic knowledge of control theory and control systems for building equipment automation required for work in building environment and energy application engineering. This lays the foundation for further courses in the field of automatic control, analysis, and design of HVAC automatic control systems. The main content of the course includes: Introduction to Automatic Control Principles, including basic concepts and components of systems; The mathematical model of the control system, including Laplace transform, transfer function, and block diagram; The time-domain analysis method of control systems, including the transition process, stability, and steady-state error of first-order and second-order systems; Root locus method, including root locus principle, basic rules, and application analysis; Frequency characteristic method, including the drawing method of frequency characteristic curve, Nyquist stability criterion, and control system performance analysis; Synthesis and calibration of control systems. Students are required to focus on mastering Laplace transform, as well as three types of analysis methods: time-domain analysis, root locus method, and frequency characteristic method. They are able to flexibly apply the above methods to analyze the stability, accuracy, and dynamic performance of control systems, and master the correction characteristics of speed measurement feedback and PID control on the system. The course is taught through methods such as lectures, classroom assignments, interactive discussions, offline Q&A, and online practice. The course assessment results are cumulative, and the assessment process includes homework, classroom assignments, and final exams. The total assessment score is calculated as a percentage, with a maximum score of 100 points.'
