    en: Space solar cells are core components of the power systems in modern spacecraft, playing a critical role in energy conversion during space missions. However, the harsh space environment—including extreme temperature variations, vacuum conditions, particle radiation, ultraviolet radiation, atomic oxygen, and more—poses significant challenges to the performance and lifespan of these solar cells. This course covers the fundamental theories of space solar cells, the characteristics of the space environment, the mechanisms through which environmental factors affect solar cell performance, as well as performance evaluation and reliability testing methods. By analyzing orbital data, conducting simulation experiments, and examining case studies, students will learn to assess the specific impacts of the space environment on space solar cells and develop strategies to enhance device stability and reliability. With a strong emphasis on integrating theory with practice, the course offers students the opportunity to engage with the "National Major Science and Technology Infrastructure – Ground-based Space Environment Simulation Facility," where they will utilize professional equipment to test and analyze photoelectric devices. Upon completion of the course, students will be proficient in identifying and analyzing various space environmental effects, mastering key techniques for evaluating the performance of photoelectric devices, and laying a solid technical foundation for the design and execution of future space missions.
