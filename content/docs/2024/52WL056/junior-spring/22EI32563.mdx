    en: Real-time computing plays a crucial role in today's society because more and more complex systems rely on computing system control. Application scenarios include nuclear power plants, railway turnout conversion systems, automotive and avionics systems, air traffic control, telecommunications, robotics, and military systems. Recently, real-time computing has been applied in new application fields such as medical devices, consumer electronics, multimedia systems, flight simulation systems, virtual reality, and interactive games. Despite its wide range of applications, most current real-time systems still use low-level programming and experiential techniques for design and implementation, lacking scientific methodological support. This method leads to a lack of reliability and may lead to serious environmental damage or even loss of life in critical applications. This course focuses on the foundation of real-time computing and emphasizes predictable scheduling algorithms. The main purpose is to teach the basic concepts of real-time computing, the most important research results in this field, and provide basic methods for designing predictable computing systems to support critical control applications.
