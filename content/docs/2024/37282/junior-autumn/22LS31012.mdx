    en: The exponential growth of big biological data has led biological research to evolve towards a data-driven direction, meeting the urgent demand for the effective utilization of vast data information and exploration of biological issues. The application and development of computational methods, along with comprehensive resource sharing, are critical for achieving this goal. As an emerging discipline, computational biology quantifies and refines traditional life sciences research, enabling observation and study at the systemic level. It has permeated various aspects of biological and medical research fields. This course aims to provide students with comprehensive knowledge and skills in computational biology, covering multiple important topics and research directions in this field. These include high-throughput data analysis, single-cell sequencing data analysis, computational proteomics, computational epigenetics, computational oncology, computational immunology, computational methods and applications in drug design, imaging omics, and artificial intelligence. Students will learn how to process and analyze large-scale biological data, including genomic, transcriptomic, and proteomic data at different levels. They will explore the role of computational biology tools and techniques in understanding gene regulation and disease occurrence. Additionally, they will learn how to predict and optimize drug activity, selectivity, and toxicity using computational methods, and design new drug molecules to treat various diseases. The computer laboratory sessions aim to introduce the fundamental methods and applications of gene expression data analysis, cancer survival analysis, and protein network analysis in the field of computational biology. Through a combination of theoretical lectures, practical demonstrations, and case studies, students will learn how to acquire gene expression and clinical data from public databases such as GEO and TCGA. They will master the methods of gene differential expression analysis using GEO2R software, apply survival analysis techniques to evaluate the association between genes and clinical phenotypes, and acquire skills in protein network analysis using the STRING database. Additionally, they will learn the R language for data analysis and visualization. Through hands-on experiments and practical projects, students will have the opportunity to apply the methods learned to real biological research, cultivating their data analysis and scientific research capabilities.
