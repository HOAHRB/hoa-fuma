The Solid-State Electron Theory Experiment is an experimental teaching course that is compatible with the core course "Solid-State Electrons" for undergraduates of the Department of Microelectronics Science and Technology. The purpose of the program is to enable students to become familiar with the basic theories of semiconductor physics and the main properties of semiconductors through experiments, so as to lay the necessary foundation for the study of semiconductor devices, sensors and applications, integrated circuits and other professional courses in the future, as well as for students to engage in electronic science and technology in the future. Practice has proved that the course has achieved very satisfactory results. Microelectronic Devices and Process Experiment is an experimental course covering two courses: "Principles of Microelectronic Devices" and "Microelectronic Technology". Under the guidance of the teacher, students are required to preview before class, learn to use experimental instruments, complete the experimental operation content by themselves, analyze experimental phenomena, process data, and write experimental reports. Through the study of this course, students will master the testing and experimental principles and methods of the performance of important components of microelectronics and their key process parameters, be familiar with the commonly used testing instruments and equipment in the microelectronics industry, and deepen their understanding of the theory of microelectronics technology.
