Their unique electrical and optical properties are the fundamental premise for various electronic systems such as integrated circuits, semiconductor devices, and optoelectronic equipment to realize their functions. They have been widely penetrated into various fields such as communication, energy, medical care, and artificial intelligence, becoming a key support for promoting technological progress and industrial upgrading. At present, semiconductor technology is developing rapidly towards the nanometer level and multi-functionalization, putting forward higher requirements for the basic theoretical literacy and practical application ability of practitioners. Taking this classic textbook as the core, the course "Semiconductor Physics" closely focuses on the basic theories and practical applications of semiconductor physics, comprehensively introducing core knowledge such as electronic states in semiconductors, impurity and defect energy levels, statistical distribution and transport laws of carriers, characteristics of non-equilibrium carriers, p-n junctions, and semiconductor photoelectric and magnetic effects. It balances theoretical depth and practical orientation, correctly elaborates core physical concepts, and assists with necessary mathematical derivations to help students establish a clear semiconductor physical image. The course aims to enable students to systematically master the basic concepts, theories and research methods of semiconductor physics, deeply understand the working mechanism of semiconductor devices, establish the connection between theory and practical application, improve the ability to solve practical problems related to semiconductors, and lay a solid foundation for subsequent learning of related courses such as integrated circuits and semiconductor device design and manufacturing, as well as future engagement in scientific research, development and application work in the semiconductor field.
