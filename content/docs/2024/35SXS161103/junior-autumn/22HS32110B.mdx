    en: The Sociology for Demography, or Sociology of Population, is a sub-field of sociology, focusing on population phenomena such as size, composition, distribution, growth, movement, and change. It seeks to uncover the principles of population issues and its developmental changes, exploring the relationship between population development and social evolution. The course aims to equip students with the foundational theories and knowledge of the Sociology of Population, further nurturing and enhancing their theoretical understanding of sociology and their research capabilities. The course ensures students are well-informed about current global population development trends and nurtures their capability to apply sociological theories to interpret social phenomena, subsequently proposing practical solutions. Two main perspectives are stressed here. First, the Perspective of Structural Differences. The heterogeneity or variability of populations is fundamental to all social sciences. By employing population thinking, the discipline studies and comprehends general phenomena based on differences in group or individual characteristics. Second, the Demographic Transition Perspective. The natural reproduction of a population follows its own principles of change, such as demographic transitions, cyclical population changes, inertia of population growth, etc. All of these require a long-term time dimension for investigation. In studies of social transitions, scholars in this field offer insights through cohort analysis and the concept of the life course.
