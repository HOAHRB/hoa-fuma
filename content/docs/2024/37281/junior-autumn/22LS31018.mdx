    en: 'This course is grounded in the core theories of modern immunology and cutting-edge clinical immunotherapy technologies. It systematically explains fundamental mechanisms such as immune recognition, immune response, and immune regulation, while integrating applied technologies including antibody drugs, cell therapy, and vaccine development. Balancing basic scientific principles with clinical practicality, the course is suitable as an introduction to study and research for students in medicine, biomedicine, pharmacy, and related fields. Beginning with the structure and function of the immune system, the course sequentially introduces the basic characteristics of immune organs, immune cells, and immune molecules. It focuses on elucidating the response patterns of innate and adaptive immunity, including antigen recognition, lymphocyte activation, antibody production, cellular immune effects, and the mechanisms underlying immune memory formation, helping learners establish a comprehensive logical framework for immune responses. At the same time, it provides an in-depth explanation of the signaling pathways of key molecules such as MHC molecules, antigen presentation, cytokines, and the complement system, laying a theoretical foundation for understanding immune-related diseases and therapeutic technologies. Building on this foundation, the course focuses on the core content of immunotherapy, systematically introducing current mainstream clinical applications and research directions: including monoclonal antibody drugs, antibody-drug conjugates, immune checkpoint inhibitors, adoptive cell immunotherapy (CAR-T, TCR-T), tumor vaccines, gene-edited immunotherapy, and treatment strategies for anti-infectious immunity and autoimmune diseases. Using clinical case studies, the course analyzes the mechanisms of action, applicable scenarios, advantages, and limitations of immunotherapy, highlighting the pathway from basic theory to clinical translation. The course emphasizes the integration of mechanisms and applications, covering both fundamental concepts such as immune tolerance, immune regulation, inflammation, and immunopathology, as well as applied fields including tumor immunology, infection immunology, autoimmune diseases, and transplant immunology. By tracing the development of immunotherapy, the course helps learners understand the scientific principles and R&D logic behind landmark technologies such as PD-1/PD-L1, CTLA-4, and CD19-CAR, establishing a coherent understanding of the “fundamental mechanisms—drug development—clinical efficacy” continuum. This course aims to equip learners with a core framework of immunological knowledge, an understanding of the key technical principles of immunotherapy, and insight into cutting-edge industry advancements and research approaches. It seeks to enhance their ability to apply theoretical knowledge and develop scientific research thinking, thereby providing a solid foundation for those engaged in basic research, drug development, clinical diagnosis and treatment, and the biopharmaceutical industry.'
