    en: Multiscale Structural Biology and Molecular Machines explores how life works at every level—from atoms and molecules to whole cells. We study the structure, motion, and function of biomolecules and uncover how molecular machines operate so efficiently. This course encourages students to think across scales, combining ideas from physics, chemistry, and biology, and to approach life sciences in a quantitative, interdisciplinary way. We cover both the basics and the latest discoveries. Topics include the 3D structures and folding of proteins and nucleic acids; methods like X-ray crystallography, cryo-EM, NMR, and molecular simulations; key molecular machines such as motor proteins, ATP synthase, ribosomes, and chaperones; membrane channels, transporters, and GPCRs; and how these machines work together in processes like muscle contraction, intracellular transport, and nerve signaling. We’ll also introduce advanced tools like single-molecule experiments, super-resolution microscopy, and nanoscale biosensors. By the end of the course, students will know how to use modern physical, chemical, and computational tools to study biomolecules and molecular machines. They will learn to analyze biological systems from a multiscale perspective and think across disciplines, improving their ability to ask smart questions, analyze problems, and develop innovative solutions.
