    en: This neuroscience and brain science course is for Bachelor students. The course starts with an introduction to the different cell types of the brain, i.e. neurons and glia (astrocytes, microglia and oligodendrocytes), emphasizing their structure and specific functions. After discussing mechanisms that establish the resting membrane potential, specific and unique features of neurons such as the ability to inter-convert electrical signals and chemical signals for communication, and the underlying cellular mechanisms and proteins, will be discussed in more depth. This is followed by a chapter on synaptic transmission, which covers the structure of a synapse, the seminal detection of chemical neurotransmission by Otto Loewi, an explanation of different types of neurotransmitters, and the sequential steps from synthesis to release of a neurotransmitter in the presynaptic neuron. Examples of important neurotransmitters such as acetylcholine and its action at the neuromuscular synapse required for muscle contraction will be discussed. The next chapter will introduce the overall structure of the nervous system, including its division into central and peripheral nervous system, as well as the major parts of the central nervous system (cerebrum, cerebellum, brain stem, spinal cord) with some of their specific functions and their anatomical connections. This part will emphasize the importance of neo-cortex development and differentiation as a key feature of intelligent animals and higher cognitive abilities, such as reasoning and planning. Introduction of Chemical Senses, Circadian Rhythms and Sleep and Brain Mechanisms of Emotion. The latter will lead over to an introduction and discussion of frequent Mental Illnesses such as depression and anxiety, their neurochemical abnormalities and current treatments. Towards the end of the course students will learn about “Memory Systems” and “Molecular Mechanisms of Learning and Memory”, which cover some of the key molecular and structural changes that occur in neurons when memory is formed and recalled in the brain. “Alzheimer’s disease” and patients with brain damage to specific parts of their brain will be introduced to illustrate how defects in brain memory systems cause dementia (memory loss).
