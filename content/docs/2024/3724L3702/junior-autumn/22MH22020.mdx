    en: Principles of Brain-Computer Interfaces is an elective course offered by the School of Medicine and Health, designed for students majoring in Biomedical Engineering, Intelligent Medical Engineering, and related disciplines. This course situates itself at the intersection of neuroscience, information science, and clinical medicine, providing a systematic introduction to the fundamental principles, key technologies, and innovative applications of brain-computer interfaces (BCI). It aims to cultivate students' interdisciplinary systems thinking and engineering practice capabilities, laying a solid foundation for future research or engineering development in neural engineering, rehabilitation medicine, intelligent medical devices, and other related fields. The curriculum covers neurophysiological fundamentals, biosignal acquisition and processing, decoding algorithms, and the design of application systems. Students will first learn the basic mechanisms of brain structure and neural signal generation, including the characteristics and acquisition methods of multimodal neural signals such as electroencephalography (EEG) and electrocorticography (ECoG). Building on this foundation, the course emphasizes key processing techniques such as signal preprocessing, feature extraction, and pattern recognition—particularly machine learning-based neural signal decoding methods—enabling students to master the complete pipeline from raw signal processing to control command generation. After establishing a firm grasp of the core principles and technical methodologies, the course further delves into the system architecture of BCI and its typical application scenarios. Students will examine the composition and performance characteristics of various BCI systems, including non-invasive and invasive approaches, and study real-world use cases in areas such as stroke rehabilitation, motor function substitution, communication for patients with disorders of consciousness, virtual reality interaction, and cognitive enhancement. The course emphasizes the integration of theory, technology, and application, fostering a comprehensive understanding of BCI systems from underlying mechanisms to top-level design. To strengthen students’ comprehensive innovation and practical abilities, the course adopts a teaching model that combines theoretical instruction with hands-on computer practice. Students will implement signal processing and decoding algorithms using platforms such as MATLAB or Python, and attempt to design functional prototypes of simple BCI systems. Through experimental exercises and project-based learning, students will enhance their engineering capabilities in multimodal neural signal processing, decoding model construction, and system integration, gradually developing preliminary competence in designing and developing BCI solutions. In addition to technical training, the course attaches great importance to ethical awareness and social responsibility. A dedicated module guides students in discussing profound issues brought about by BCI technology, including privacy security, personal identity, social equity, and ethical boundaries. This module aims to help students establish a sense of responsibility that aligns with technical standards and social values, and to understand the balance between innovation and regulation.
