This course aims to introduce fundamental knowledge of computer vision, elucidating core concepts, historical evolution, and theoretical frameworks of traditional image analysis techniques. It will strengthen students’ foundational expertise in the field while enabling them to master basic applications of visual technologies through practical implementation. The course employs a teaching methodology centered on lectures supplemented with lab sessions. It comprises 24 hours of lecture-based instruction and 16 hours of hands-on computer lab training. Building upon the theoretical knowledge from lectures, the lab sessions enable students to master classic computer vision theories, techniques, and real-world implementations. This foundation prepares students for advanced studies and research in intelligent vision engineering. Theoretical Curriculum Includes: Imaging pipelines, quantization and aliasing theory; Image compression and fidelity; Image region segmentation; Edge contour extraction; Feature recognition and texture analysis; Image calibration and 3D reconstruction. Lab Experiments Cover: Image compression techniques; Image registration; Camera calibration and panorama stitching; 3D reconstruction workflows. Through mastering core theories and implementing corresponding algorithms in labs, students will develop a foundational grasp of computer vision principles and classical methodologies. The course broadens students’ horizons while cultivating their initial capability to address computer vision challenges.'
