Course Objectives The course aims to achieve three primary goals: To develop students’ ability to construct rigorous mathematical proofs using logical reasoning and precise language. To equip students with a solid understanding of numerical series, functional series, power series, Fourier series, and multivariate differential calculus. To foster abstract thinking and problem-solving skills by applying analytical tools to theoretical and practical problems. Course Content The curriculum is comprehensive and includes the following key topics: Convergence tests for numerical series (positive, alternating, absolute, conditional) Concepts of pointwise and uniform convergence for sequences and series of functions Properties and applications of power series and Taylor expansions Fourier series: definitions, convergence modes, and applications Introduction to metric spaces, normed spaces, and Banach spaces Limits, continuity, differentiability, and extremum problems for multivariate functions Implicit and inverse function theorems Each topic is taught through lectures supplemented with problem-solving sessions, class discussions, and regular assessments to ensure conceptual clarity and practical proficiency.
