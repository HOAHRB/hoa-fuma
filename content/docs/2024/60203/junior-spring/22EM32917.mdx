To advance China''s 2035 carbon peak and 2060 carbon neutrality goals, the School of Economics and Management offers this expert-led course, Future City Management: Energy System and Climate Change. It is designed to cultivate interdisciplinary talent with robust analytical skills and literacy in low-carbon governance. This course is designed for undergraduate students from diverse backgrounds including economics and management, energy, environment, transportation, architecture, and civil engineering. It aims to guide students in deeply understanding the complex interrelationships between urban energy systems, carbon emission accounting, climate change, and sustainable development. The curriculum is closely aligned with national strategies and academic frontiers, structured around five core modules: 1.Energy System Fundamentals: Analyzing the composition and flexibility characteristics of future urban clean energy systems, and examining energy supply-demand response mechanisms. 2.Carbon Footprint Accounting Methods: Focusing on quantitative tools like Input-Output Analysis and Structural Decomposition Analysis, guiding students to perform basic carbon emission accounting at regional or sectoral levels. 3.Linkages between Economic Growth and Carbon Emissions: Exploring the dynamic relationship between energy consumption and carbon emissions under China''s new economic normal, and identifying key influencing factors. 4.Sustainable Urbanization Governance: Systematically introducing theories and methods of urban ecosystem management from spatiotemporal dimensions, and interpreting the policy frameworks and implementation pathways for low-carbon urbanization. 5.Energy-Water-Food-Waste Nexus System: Focusing on synergistic resource management within the context of smart cities, and discussing the application scenarios of low-carbon technologies in optimizing multi-system interactions. As an integrated, interdisciplinary course, it not only helps students build a solid theoretical knowledge foundation but also focuses on developing their core competencies to address the complex energy and environmental challenges facing future cities. Upon completion, students will be able to systematically understand the policy logic and international dynamics underlying the "Dual Carbon" goals, skillfully apply mainstream analytical tools for carbon accounting and energy planning, and preliminarily possess the literacy needed to participate in urban low-carbon governance decision-making, laying a solid foundation for future careers in government, enterprises, research institutions, and related fields.'
