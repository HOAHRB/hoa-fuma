Research tasks involve studying strength, stiffness, and stability. The syllabus of this course is formulated from the understanding that the mechanical analysis methods of deformable objects have nothing to do with the geometry of deformable objects. The basic concepts of the course include strength, stiffness, stability, stress, strain, strain energy, etc. The basic principles and theories include mutual equivalence of shear stress, equilibrium, continuity of strain and coordination of deformation, stress-strain relationship, energy principle, mutual equivalence theorem, strength theory, superposition principle, etc. The primary approach involves solving mechanical problems of deformation on the basis of the analysis of equilibrium, geometry and physics. This course strengthens the main line of stress and strain analysis, highlights the three major equations of mechanics, geometry and physics, attaches equal importance to analytical solution and numerical simulation, and aims to cultivate students' solid basic knowledge and ability of creative thinking, mathematical modeling of engineering structure physics, complex system analysis, and multidisciplinary comprehensive design and engineering practice.
