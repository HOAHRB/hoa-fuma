    en: Public finance is an important branch of modern economics, focusing on the economic behaviors of the government. Taking market failure as the starting point, the course clarifies the functions of the government and covers contents such as resource allocation, income distribution, and economic stability. Through the study of aspects like public expenditures (such as education and social security expenditures), public revenues (such as taxes and national debts), and public choices (such as the government decision-making mechanism), students are enabled to understand the government's revenue and expenditure activities and their impacts on the economy. This course not only attaches importance to the construction of the theoretical system, deriving the functions of the government starting from the assumption of a perfectly competitive market; but also emphasizes the connection between theory and practice, guiding students to analyze real-world fiscal phenomena and laying a foundation for them to engage in work in the fields of economics and public finance or conduct further research.
