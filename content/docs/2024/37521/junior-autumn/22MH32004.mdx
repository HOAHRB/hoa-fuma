    en: Molecular imaging refers to the interdisciplinary field that utilizes imaging methods and technological approaches to visualize, qualify, and quantitatively study cellular and molecular-level biological processes in living organisms, including humans and animals. In comparison to traditional medical imaging, molecular imaging focuses on the fundamental changes at the cellular and molecular levels in the pathophysiological processes of living organisms. Through anatomical, functional, metabolic, or molecular information, it provides robust support for the visual representation of pathological physiological processes such as gene expression, protein interactions, signal transduction, and cellular metabolism. Molecular imaging enables qualitative or quantitative analysis and capturing of pathophysiological processes, establishing a solid foundation for the study of disease occurrence, progression, and outcomes. Building upon prerequisites in biochemistry, molecular biology, general chemistry, and introductory courses in medicine, this course systematically introduces the design principles of molecular imaging probes and their practical applications in magnetic resonance imaging (MRI), computed tomography (CT), nuclear medicine, ultrasound, and optical imaging. As a significant outcome of interdisciplinary collaboration between medicine and engineering, the Molecular Imaging course, from a practical application perspective, enhances students' understanding of and insight into the forefront of biomedical engineering. It also helps students exercise their ability to comprehensively apply, integrate, and synthesize knowledge from foundational disciplines such as biochemistry, molecular biology, general chemistry, and medical imaging. The course aims to strengthen students' capacity to analyze and address problems using a multidisciplinary knowledge background.
