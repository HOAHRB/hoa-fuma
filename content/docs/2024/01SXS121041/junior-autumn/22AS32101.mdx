    en: “Flight Dynamics” is a specialized course for engineering students who are major in Control Science and Engineering or Science and Technology in Aeronautics and Astronautics in technical universities. Via lectures and computer experiments, the purpose of this course is to train the students to have the capability of establishing dynamic mathematical models to describe the motions of flight vehicles via using knowledge on advanced mathematics, theoretical mechanics, and aerodynamics. The typical background of this course is that an endo-atmospheric interceptor missile intercepting a maneuvering target. Based on the motion of this interceptor missile, some normal coordinates and coordinates transformations in flight dynamics are introduced. Then the properties and mathematical descriptions of aerodynamic forces and the moments yielded by aerodynamic forces are given. Next, the properties and mathematical descriptions of reaction propulsion and its moments are presented. It follows that the properties of aerodynamic disturbance effect on side direction reaction propulsion are analyzed and a mathematical description for this effect is given. Moreover, the gravity on the missile is studied and mathematically depicted. Using the above results, equations to describe the missile motion are derived from the principles of Newton’s mechanics. Via studying the above knowledge on flight dynamics, the students will master the fundamental principle and method of modeling the flight motion and so have the capability of deriving motion equations for any type of flight vehicles. In computer simulation experiments, the students will design maneuvering flight paths of an aircraft, such that they are able to obtain numerical solutions for flight motions.
