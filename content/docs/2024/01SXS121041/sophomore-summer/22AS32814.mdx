    en: 'This course is aimed at sophomore students majoring in microelectronics science and engineering. It mainly introduces optoelectronic microsystems, an advanced technology of microelectronics in the post-Moorish era, so that students can understand and master the frontier progress in related fields. This course adopts the teaching methods of classroom teaching and classroom group discussion, combined with examples of scientific research projects, in order to fully stimulate students'' interest in learning and broaden their horizons. This course begins with a brief introduction of optoelectronic microsystems, teaching the basic concepts, characteristics, development history and typical applications in various fields, and outlining the technical bottlenecks faced by optoelectronic microsystems today. This course focuses on several key technologies involved in optoelectronic microsystems: photodetectors, silicon photonic chips, and heterogeneous integration technologies. First of all, the photodetector is introduced as a bridge between light and electricity, explaining various materials, device structure, detection architecture and device function and readout circuit, combined with the advantages of the CETC 11th Institute to describe the fourth generation of focal plane technology. Secondly, silicon photonic chips are introduced, including the basic concepts, functions, principles and development trends of optical waveguide, modulator, coupler, optical generation module and optical collection integrated module. Thirdly, it focuses on the essential packaging and integration technology of optoelectronic microsystem, introducing the chiplet concept, chip level packaging, system level packaging, multi-chip module, 2D/3D heterogeneous integration architecture and various software heterogeneous integration methods. Finally, the semiconductor IDM mode is summarized, and the domestic and foreign layout of photoelectric technology is introduced. Through the study of this course, students will form a preliminary cognition of optoelectronic microsystem technology, and their scientific research quality will be improved to a certain extent.'
