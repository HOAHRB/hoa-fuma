    en: Semiconductor physics is the core foundation of semiconductor technology and semiconductor industry, which has developed rapidly in recent years. As a basic technical course with an application background, this course takes the electronic states in semiconductors, impurity and defect energy levels in semiconductors, and the statistical distribution of carriers in semiconductors as the basic theory, and provides an in-depth discussion of the drifting motion of carriers in semiconductors and its effect on the conductive properties, the basic physical processes of non-equilibrium carrier injection and compounding. The course then focuses on the ideal PN junction model and its current-voltage equations in a non-equilibrium state, starting from the formation of semiconductor PN junctions and the basic physical mechanisms such as impurity charge distribution and the PN junction energy band diagram, so as to analyze the various factors affecting the deviation of the PN junction current-voltage characteristics from the ideal equations. The task of this course is to enable students to master the basic concepts, theories and methods of the subject of semiconductor physics, and to gradually improve their understanding, reasoning, analyzing and computational skills of semiconductor physics problems. The basic concepts and theories of semiconductors are emphasized in terms of simplicity and depth, and the teaching of theoretical methods and problem analysis is focused on the clarity of theory and the specificity of problems. Students are required to have a thorough understanding and profound grasp of the organic fusion of carriers and PN junctions in semiconductors based on the fundamental theory of semiconductor physics. Through the focus on typical problems, students will be able to develop the ability to learn and study semiconductor physics in depth. This will provide students with the theoretical foundation for research and teaching in optoelectronic information science and engineering and for the development of new semiconductor devices.
