    en: 'Export Management: Theory and Practice is a 1-credit (16-hour) optional course offered by the School of Management at Harbin Institute of Technology (HIT). The course is jointly taught by Professor Gracy J.Y. Yang from the University of Sydney Business School and Assistant Professor Yumeng Luo from HIT’s School of Management, adopting a bilingual teaching approach. The course aims to equip students with a systematic understanding of core theories in corporate export management, practical operational procedures across the entire export lifecycle, and cutting-edge industry trends. Furthermore, it seeks to cultivate students’ comprehensive capabilities in formulating and implementing effective export strategies for enterprises within a complex and dynamic international environment. The curriculum is structured closely around the real-world chain of corporate export operations, adopting a dual-module design of “Practical Foundations + Strategic Expansion” that follows the logical progression of “Theoretical Foundations – Operational Execution – Risk and Expansion,” delivered through a relay teaching model by HIT faculty and the overseas expert. The first four sessions focus on the “Core Practical Foundations” module, taught in Chinese by the HIT faculty, keeping eyes on the key operational aspects of export business. This module systematically covers the principles of international payment methods and trade finance instruments, along with their risk comparisons; the operational logic, platform selection, and pricing models of cross-border e-commerce; the essentials and practical application of International Commercial Terms; export documentation management and freight logistics coordination; as well as export risk management and performance evaluation, thereby solidly strengthening students’ practical operational foundations. The subsequent four sessions comprise the “Strategic Preparation and Advanced Expansion” module, taught in English by the overseas expert. Drawing on real international cases, this module centers on the front-end decision-making and underlying logic of corporate exporting, covering: analysis of firms’ export motivations and barriers, export readiness assessment, overseas market selection and entry mode comparisons, the establishment and management of international channel partnerships, as well as cutting-edge topics such as the legal dimensions of international sales contracts, intellectual property protection, and grey market responses, with a focus on frontier areas including cross-border e-commerce, service exports, and sustainable trade. The course emphasizes the principles of “learning by doing and doing by learning,” employing an integrated teaching model that combines case studies, situational simulations, and group presentations. Students will work in teams to select a real firm or product and undertake full-process training spanning export strategy planning, market analysis, and operational plan development, culminating in a final presentation delivered entirely in English. The course assessment is based on the group presentation, which evaluates students’ ability to apply theoretical tools such as payment methods and market entry modes to solve real-world export challenges, as well as their cross-cultural communication and teamwork skills. This course lays a solid foundation for students pursuing future careers in multinational operations, international business consulting, and related management fields, while equipping them as high-caliber professionals to support Chinese enterprises in their “going global” endeavors.'
