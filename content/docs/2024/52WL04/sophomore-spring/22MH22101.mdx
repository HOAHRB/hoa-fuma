    en: Introduction to Medicine (1) is an integrated course in medicine. This course includes the study of human anatomy, histology and embryology, physiology, and pathology in basic medicine. Human anatomy provides a macroscopic introduction to the structure of the human body, including the distribution of skeletal, muscular, nervous, and vascular systems. Histology and embryology focus on the microscopic study of human tissue structure, embryonic development, and common congenital malformations. Physiology emphasizes the normal functional activities of various organs and systems in the human body. Pathology introduces the macroscopic and microscopic pathological changes in tissues and organs caused by diseases. The learning process involves understanding the normal structure and function of the human body before grasping the pathological changes in tissues and organs caused by diseases. Assignments given before class guide students to learn independently and develop their skills in information retrieval and literature review. Case-based teaching introduces common diseases in daily life before lecturing, inspiring students to think before learning, guiding them to learn with questions, and enabling them to apply what they have learned in practice. During the teaching process, attention is paid to the cultivation of students’ abilities, introducing engineering-related technologies applied in medicine, gradually developing students’ research thinking to solve medical bottlenecks through engineering technology. The course incorporates ideological and humanistic education, inspiring students to understand the human body as an organic whole, the coordinated functions of various organs and systems in the body to maintain homeostasis, and the objective laws of disease development. It also helps students recognize pathology as the “gold standard” for diagnosing diseases. Post-class assignments focus on cultivating students’ ability for independent learning, expanding their knowledge of cutting-edge medicine and research progress. The education aims to instill in students a sense of awe for life, respect for life, a spirit of diligent study, and a willingness to dedicate themselves like craftsmen.
