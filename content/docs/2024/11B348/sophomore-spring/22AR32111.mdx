    en: This course aims to provide an in-depth understanding of the development process of foreign cities. It explores the urban regional characteristics of major civilizations and their horizontal exchanges, while emphasizing the vertical urban revolution from agricultural civilization to industrial civilization. Additionally, it examines the impact of material civilization, institutional civilization, and spiritual civilization on cities at various levels. By adopting a broad perspective on civilizational history, students are encouraged to contemplate the significant achievements made by cities and explore the driving factors behind urban development. Furthermore, they will gain insights into the complexity of cities through an examination of different subsystems and elements that interact within them. Through analyzing diverse urban forms, students will learn how to interpret cities and comprehend how urban layouts influence architecture as well as how architectural construction shapes urban spaces. The origin and subsequent influence of "ideal city" concepts in Western city history will be analyzed to help students understand both its significance and potential dangers associated with utopian ideologies. Discussions will enable students to reflect upon and critique existing issues within contemporary cities while also analyzing challenges and opportunities brought about by modern scientific and technological revolutions for their future development. Moreover, this course seeks to cultivate an appreciation for the beauty inherent in cities while stimulating a love for traditional ones thus awakening awareness regarding historical preservation efforts. Ultimately, this course aims to establish a correct concept regarding urban development while simultaneously training students' critical thinking abilities so that they can effectively analyze both gains achieved as well as losses incurred during foreign urban developments.
