    en: This course aims to explore diverse perspectives on the definition and origin of cities, introduce the earliest Neolithic cities and their formation characteristics, teach the origin and development process of Chinese cities, summarize the regional characteristics and inter-regional exchanges of different cities in Chinese civilization horizontally, and emphasize the agricultural revolution, changes during the Zhou and Qin Dynasties, transformations in Tang Song Dynasties, as well as urban revolution caused by globalization vertically. It encompasses the influence of material civilization, system civilization, and spiritual civilization on urban areas. The objective is to guide students in contemplating large-scale civilizational achievements of cities within a broad framework of civilizational history while comprehending city complexity through interaction mechanisms among various subsystems and elements. This paper introduces two significant urban planning concepts along with their temporal and spatial backgrounds during pre-Qin period. By interpreting different urban forms, students can learn how to analyze cities while tracing migration processes from "Yellow River Era" to "Canal era" in Chinese capital's history; thus enabling them to understand complex political, military, and economic considerations reflected in capital city locations. The discussion prompts students to reflect upon existing issues within cities while analyzing challenges as well as opportunities brought about by modern scientific and technological revolutions for contemporary urban development. Students are guided towards appreciating city aesthetics which stimulates love for traditional cities while awakening awareness regarding historical-cultural preservation.
