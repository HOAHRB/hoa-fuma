The course systematically introduces the structure, operation mechanisms, and functions of major international organizations such as the United Nations, the World Trade Organization, and the International Monetary Fund. It elaborates on the basic concepts of global governance, its subjects, objects, and main models, and narrates the development and evolution of global governance theories and their main schools. It also analyzes the theoretical paradigms of global governance (such as realism, liberalism, and constructivism) and practical challenges (anti-globalization, trade frictions, climate change, transnational conflicts, public health crises, etc.). Through teaching methods such as classroom lectures and case studies, the course aims to cultivate students'' ability to analyze the policy impacts of international organizations, evaluate the effectiveness of global governance, and develop innovative and critical thinking to solve practical problems. It also strengthens students'' global perspective and international responsibility awareness, helping them to deal with complex global issues and laying a foundation for their future participation in international affairs and global governance work.
