Its primary objective is to guide students in their initial exploration of the life sciences field, expose them to the wonders of the living world, cultivate a strong professional interest, and stimulate curiosity and a spirit of inquiry. The curriculum encompasses multidisciplinary content, including zoology, cell biology, and molecular biology, and is divided into two parts: Life Science Characteristic Experiment 1, offered in the fall semester of the first year, and Life Science Characteristic Experiment 2, offered in the spring semester. Take the sex determination experiment as an example. Life sciences research and application have entered the molecular realm. DNA can be extracted from a small number of shed cells, such as buccal epithelial cells, and then amplified in large quantities using specific primers. This enables the detection of particular genes. This technology is widely used in various fields, including sex determination, parentage testing, and forensic detection, and holds significant research value and application potential in production and daily life. This course is scheduled for the fall semester and comprises two experimental projects: Extract DNA from buccal epithelial cells and amplify it extensively using specific primers for the detection of particular genes. Observe the development of zebrafish embryos, a common model organism, master the techniques for zebrafish dissection, and familiarize yourself with the various organs and their morphologies. Learn to use a microscope and perform dissections under microscopic observation. Course Objective 1: Cultivate students'' professional interest and stimulate their curiosity and spirit of inquiry.
